# Assessment

Evaluate demonstrated performance transparently and separate evidence from inference.

## Process

1. Identify the learning target, task, submitted response, and available criteria. State when a rubric, reference answer, or required assumptions are missing.
2. Determine the evidence needed for a valid judgment. Do not score presentation quality as subject mastery unless the criteria require it.
3. Analyze the response independently. Use `stem-reasoning` when correctness depends on a derivation, proof, calculation, algorithm, or technical assumption.
4. Compare the response with the criteria step by step. Credit correct reasoning even when the final answer is wrong, and identify unsupported correct guesses.
5. Classify issues as conceptual misunderstanding, prerequisite gap, procedural error, calculation error, incomplete justification, or communication ambiguity.
6. Assign a score or performance level only when criteria support one. Explain each deduction or uncertainty.
7. Provide feedback that identifies strengths, the highest-impact correction, and a next action.
8. When assessing understanding, propose a transfer or retrieval check rather than generalizing from one response.

## Assessment modes

### Answer check

Judge correctness and sufficiency. Show the decisive evidence and avoid expanding into a full lesson unless requested.

### Solution review

Evaluate the method, assumptions, intermediate steps, final result, and verification. Distinguish a flawed method from an isolated execution error.

### Understanding check

Evaluate explanation, transfer, and ability to distinguish nearby concepts. Use multiple pieces of evidence before making broad claims about mastery.

## Output

Include, as applicable:

- target and criteria
- judgment or score with confidence
- evidence from the response
- strengths
- errors or missing evidence by category
- concise corrective feedback
- next assessment or learning action

Coordinate with `education-learning` for an interactive feedback loop. Never invent rubric requirements, learner history, or evidence not present in the submission.
