# Structured Writing

Create purposeful prose from material the user has supplied or approved. Keep the writing process proportional to the document's stakes and preserve the evidence state of every claim.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the writing contract

Determine from the request and Project context:

- purpose and intended reader;
- genre, target language, tone, and desired length;
- required content, prohibited content, style guidance, and delivery constraints;
- which sources are authoritative and which wording, quotations, facts, or citations are protected;
- whether the user wants an outline, a section, a complete draft, or an iterative coauthoring process.

Infer harmless details from context. Ask only when ambiguity would materially change the document or authorize unsupported content.

### 2. Build a grounded content inventory

Collect the claims, evidence, examples, definitions, quotations, citations, decisions, and constraints available for the draft. Keep these states distinct:

- supported by an inspected source;
- supplied by the user but not independently verified;
- author or source claim;
- reasonable framing or transition;
- missing information that needs a placeholder or decision.

Use `knowledge-extraction` first when substantial structured source material must be converted into reusable content units. Never turn an inference or plausible detail into a sourced fact.

### 3. Design the document spine

State the central reader takeaway, then assign a clear job to each section. Choose an order that serves the genre and reader: answer-first, chronological, problem–analysis–recommendation, claim–evidence–implication, tutorial sequence, or another justified structure.

Check that every required item has a home, sections do not duplicate one another, and the proposed depth matches the length. Do not expose an outline as a separate deliverable unless it helps the user or was requested.

### 4. Draft with source fidelity

- Lead each section and paragraph with its useful point rather than process narration.
- Connect evidence to the claim it supports and preserve citation identity and proximity.
- Keep quotations exact and visibly quoted; do not invent locators.
- Preserve technical meaning, equations, code, names, numbers, dates, and defined terms.
- Match the requested language and the user's usable voice evidence.
- Mark material gaps explicitly, such as `[TODO: confirm sample size]`, when a complete sentence would require invention.
- Prefer concrete, economical prose, but retain necessary nuance and qualifications.

Use `stem-reasoning` only when genuine technical reasoning is required. A fluent explanation is not evidence. Use `visualization` only when a diagram or chart communicates an important relationship better than prose or a compact table.

### 5. Run a reader test

Review the draft from the intended reader's perspective:

1. Is the purpose or answer apparent soon enough?
2. Does each section perform a distinct job?
3. Can the reader follow the logic without unstated prerequisites?
4. Does each material claim have the support the draft implies?
5. Are uncertainty, limitations, and missing information visible?
6. Does the artifact satisfy its language, length, genre, and format constraints?

Revise material defects found by this test. Do not expand the scope merely to make the document look comprehensive.

### 6. Deliver the artifact

Use Markdown as the canonical editable text unless the task requires another source format. Keep the artifact in the external Project. Report consequential assumptions, unresolved gaps, or requested decisions concisely; do not attach a long process log by default.

## Boundaries

- Use `writing-revision` when the primary object is existing prose that must be revised, edited, proofread, or adapted.
- Use `document-understanding` before this Skill when a raw PDF, DOCX, slide deck, image, or other complex input needs faithful structural recovery.
- Route literature discovery, paper analysis, and cross-source evidence synthesis to the Research Agent. Do not manufacture upstream research inside a writing task.
- Do not perform document layout, publishing, Zotero writes, or Obsidian writes as part of this Skill.
- Do not preload unrelated style rules or apply a universal voice. Project instructions and the user's writing contract take precedence.
