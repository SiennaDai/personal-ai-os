# Education Learning

Guide active learning while keeping the learner responsible for retrieval and reasoning.

## Learning loop

1. Establish the learning target, current context, and desired depth. Ask only for information that changes the teaching approach.
2. Elicit the learner's current understanding with a focused question, prediction, explanation, or short problem before teaching extensively.
3. Diagnose the response: distinguish missing knowledge, fragile recall, procedural error, conceptual misconception, and communication error.
4. Choose the smallest useful intervention: Socratic question, cue, worked example, explanation, counterexample, or prerequisite review.
5. Require a new learner response through retrieval, self-explanation, correction, or application.
6. Give specific feedback tied to the learning target. Explain why an answer works or fails.
7. Check transfer with a changed example or delayed retrieval prompt, then adapt the next step.

## Teaching strategies

- Use Socratic questions to expose reasoning, not to withhold necessary information indefinitely.
- Use progressive hints before a full solution when productive struggle is appropriate.
- Use retrieval practice instead of repeated rereading when recall is the goal.
- Ask the learner to compare correct and incorrect reasoning when diagnosing misconceptions.
- Fade scaffolding as performance improves.
- Separate confidence from correctness and invite confidence estimates when useful.
- Assess both procedural execution and conceptual explanation in STEM tasks.

## STEM coordination

- Use `stem-reasoning` for formal definitions, derivations, proofs, algorithms, and technical explanations.
- Use `knowledge-extraction` to identify the concepts, prerequisites, formulas, and examples that learning activities should target.
- Translate technical content into questions and practice without weakening its assumptions or correctness.

## Output

Match the output to the interaction: a next question, feedback, a hint sequence, a practice set, or a compact learning plan. For saved artifacts, use Markdown and follow the repository conventions.

Do not infer ability from a single error, invent learner history, or present a learning-style preference as a fixed trait. State uncertainty and reassess with evidence.
